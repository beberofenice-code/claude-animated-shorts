# "The Girl Who Dreamed the Bridge" — script v1 (2026-11-26)
Topic (user): what if you dreamed someone you know would die, it really happens, and you could save them before it does?
Format: ~60 s, ~3.7 words/s, English only, no captions. Fiction ("what if") on top of real facts.

Real facts used (Wikipedia, Broughton Suspension Bridge):
- 12 April 1831, Salford. 74 men of the 60th Rifle Corps marched four abreast across the new suspension bridge.
- They felt it bounce in time with their steps, found it funny, whistled and stepped harder; one badly forged bolt snapped.
- One end fell 16-18 ft into the Irwell (about 2 ft deep). No one died. Afterwards the army ordered troops to "break step" on bridges.
- Invented for the story: Nell, Tom, the dream, the pan. In history the bridge fell and nobody was saved by a girl.

## Voice-over
1. She dreamed her brother would die, and her dreams always came true.
2. It's 1831, and Nell's brother Tom is a soldier, marching home today over the brand new iron bridge.
3. In her dream, seventy-four men walk across it in perfect step, the bridge starts to bounce, and then it falls.
4. So Nell wakes up, and she runs. Past the market, past the mill, all the way down to the river.
5. But the column is already on the bridge, four across, every boot landing on the same beat.
6. She shouts, but seventy-four boots are louder than one girl.
7. So she grabs a tin pan and bangs it, completely off the beat.
8. The men start laughing at the girl dancing around with a pan, and it turns out it's really hard to march in time while laughing.
9. One by one they lose the rhythm. The bounce shrinks, and the chains go quiet.
10. Here's why. Every bridge has a rhythm it likes to sway at, like a kid on a swing. Tiny pushes at the perfect moment go higher and higher. Break the beat, and the swing slows down.
11. Tom made it across, and the army wrote a new rule: never cross a bridge in step.
12. So now, the best drilled soldiers in the world cross every bridge like a crowd of tourists, and Tom has never been so happy to be out of step.
