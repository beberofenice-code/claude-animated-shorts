# "The Tower That Was Saved by a War" — original script v1 (2026-11-20)
Format: ~65 s vertical cartoon 3D short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady voice, English, no captions.

## Facts (with sources)
- Construction began 1173; the tower started sinking/tilting in 1178 once the 2nd-3rd floor was reached, on a
  shallow ~3 m foundation set in soft clay, sand and shells. (Wikipedia "Leaning Tower of Pisa"; Discover Magazine;
  History.com "Why does the Leaning Tower of Pisa lean")
- Wars with Genoa, Lucca and Florence halted work for almost a century; the pause let the soil consolidate —
  modern analysis says it would have toppled had building continued. (Wikipedia; Mental Floss "Why does…lean")
- Work resumed 1272; upper floors were built with one side taller to compensate, so the tower is curved.
  Bell chamber completed c. 1372. (Wikipedia)
- 1934: Mussolini's regime called the lean an inappropriate symbol and had concrete (~200 t) injected into the base;
  the tower lurched further south. (PBS NOVA "Leaning Tower"; Practical Engineering, 2023)
- Closed to the public in 1990 as unsafe; soil extracted through inclined holes under the north (high) side; tilt
  reduced ~40-45 cm; reopened 15 Dec 2001, declared stable for centuries; the lean was deliberately kept.
  (ICE "Stabilising the Leaning Tower of Pisa"; Britannica; Wikipedia)

## Script (~241 words ≈ 65 s)
The Leaning Tower of Pisa is only still standing because its city went to war.
It's 1173, and Pisa is rich, proud, and building the fanciest bell tower in Italy.
They dig a foundation just three metres deep, right into soft, wet clay.
Floor one goes up. Perfect.
Floor two goes up. Still perfect.
Floor three goes up, and the whole tower quietly leans over.
Luckily, Pisa then got dragged into a war.
Unluckily for the builders, it lasted almost a hundred years.
And that's what saved it.
Soft clay is full of water. Ever stood still on wet sand at the beach? You sink a little, then the water squeezes out and the sand holds you.
A century of waiting let the clay firm up under all that stone.
When the builders came back, they made the new floors taller on the low side to hide the tilt, so now the tower isn't just leaning, it's bent like a banana.
In 1934, Mussolini ordered it straightened. His engineers pumped tons of concrete into the base, and the tower leaned even further.
By 1990 it was so close to falling that Italy closed it. Engineers then spent eleven years gently pulling soil out from under the high side, and the tower sank back about forty centimetres.
They could have kept going until it stood perfectly straight. They stopped on purpose.
Nobody flies to Italy to photograph a tower that stands up.
