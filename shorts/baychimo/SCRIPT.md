# "The Ship That Refused to Sink" — script v1 (2026-11-23)
Format: ~65 s vertical, ~3.7 words/s, one steady voice, our own characters and story.

## Facts (sources)
- SS Baychimo: steel-hulled cargo steamer, 230 ft, 1,322 t, built 1914 in Gothenburg as Ångermanelfven; Hudson's Bay Company from 1921, carrying furs on the Arctic route. (Wikipedia "SS Baychimo"; Discovery UK)
- 1931: trapped in pack ice near Point Barrow, Alaska. HBC flew most of the crew out; 15 men incl. Capt. Cornwell stayed and built a wooden hut to wait out the winter. (Wikipedia; Explorersweb; World of Cruising)
- 24 Nov 1931: a blizzard; afterwards the ship was gone and the crew assumed she had sunk. Days later an Inuit seal hunter reported her ~45 mi (72 km) away. The men reached her, took the most valuable furs to fly out, and abandoned her, expecting she would not survive the winter. (Wikipedia; Nunatsiaq News "Taissumani"; Manitoba Museum)
- Sightings: 1932 Leslie Melvin with a dog sled team on his way to Nome; 1933 a group of Inuit sheltered aboard from a storm (single source — Discovery UK); Nov 1939 Capt. Hugh Polson boarded her to salvage, but advancing ice floes forced him off; later sightings, last reported 1969 frozen in pack ice between Point Barrow and Icy Cape, 38 years after abandonment. (Wikipedia; Explorersweb)
- Never confirmed sunk; wreck never found. (Wikipedia; ADN)
- Drift: frozen into sea ice that moves with wind and currents, the ship was carried back and forth along the same Alaska coast for decades. (Explorersweb; Marine Insight)

## Voice-over
In 1931, a crew abandoned their ship in the Arctic, and she spent the next thirty-eight years refusing to sink.
The Baychimo is a steel cargo steamer, hauling a hold full of furs along the top of the world.
Off Alaska, the sea freezes solid around her, and she's stuck.
Most of the crew get flown home. Fifteen stay behind and build a wooden hut on shore to wait for spring.
Then a blizzard hits. When the snow finally clears, the ship is gone. The captain decides she must have sunk.
Luckily, a few days later, a seal hunter spots her forty-five miles away.
Unluckily, she's forty-five miles away.
The crew sled out, grab the most valuable furs, and leave her for good. Nobody expects her to last the winter.
The next year, a man with a dog sled watches her drift past.
The year after, a group of hunters shelter inside her from a storm.
In 1939, a captain climbs aboard to claim her, and the ice shoves him off again.
You see, Arctic ice is never still. Wind and currents keep it creeping round and round the same coast.
Ever watched a suitcase nobody picks up at the airport? It just keeps coming back around.
The Baychimo was frozen onto that carousel, her steel hull still sealed tight.
She was spotted again and again, the last time in 1969, still locked in the ice.
Nobody has ever found the wreck. So as far as anyone can prove, she still hasn't sunk.
